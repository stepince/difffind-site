Example: a revised lease clause

An illustrative before-and-after example from a lease agreement.

Before

Rent is due on the 5th of each month. A late fee of $50 applies after 10 days.

After

Rent is due on the 1st of each month. A late fee of $75 applies after 5 days. The tenant is responsible for utilities.

Exact changes

  • “5th” changed to “1st”.
  • “$50” changed to “$75”.
  • “10 days” changed to “5 days”.
  • A new sentence about utilities was added.

What semantic analysis can add

Taken together, the changes make payment due earlier, shorten the grace period, raise the late fee and add a new cost for the tenant. Optional AI analysis can summarize an impact like this; the exact changes above remain the source of truth, so you can check any explanation against them.

Compare Word Files

Exact changes and changes in meaning

A line-by-line comparison shows which words changed. It does not tell you whether a change matters. A single word such as “may” becoming “must”, or “30 days” becoming “30 business days”, can change an obligation. DiffFind first shows the exact additions, removals and changes, then offers optional semantic analysis to help you judge their meaning.

When the same phrase appears several times, DiffFind highlights the exact occurrence that changed, not the first match.

Supported Word format and what is compared

  • Supported: .docx files.
  • Compared: the document text, including paragraphs, lists and tables.
  • Not compared: images, headers, footers, footnotes and text boxes.
  • Tracked changes: compared as accepted.
  • Older .doc files: save them as .docx first.

This describes Word files only. Have a PDF? See PDF Comparison, which works differently.

Use cases

  • Contracts and leases that are revised between drafts.
  • Policies and handbooks that are updated over time.
  • Reports and proposals that several people edit.
  • Specifications and requirements documents with revisions.
  • Any document where you need to confirm exactly what was edited.

Frequently asked questions

How do I compare two Word documents?

Load the original and the revised DOCX file, then run the comparison. DiffFind compares the text of both documents and highlights every added, removed and changed passage in the Before and After views.

Can DiffFind identify both exact and semantic changes?

Yes. The lexical comparison identifies the exact changes. Optional AI semantic analysis can then help explain whether the meaning of a passage changed.

What Word formats are supported?

DiffFind compares .docx files. Older .doc files need to be saved as .docx first.

Does DiffFind compare tracked changes, images and headers?

Tracked changes are compared as accepted. Images, headers, footers, footnotes and text boxes are not compared; DiffFind compares the document text, including paragraphs, lists and tables.

Is AI required for document comparison?

No. The exact comparison works without AI. Semantic analysis is optional.
